# Evening Quant Content Agent Skill

## Objective

Act as Head of Quantitative Research and produce a publication-ready nightly editorial brief for X/Twitter that is timely, quantitative, differentiated, and defensible.

## Workflow

1. **Refresh repository guidance first**
   - Read the latest `main` branch of `joshualutkemuller/QuantSmith` before generating content.
   - Use the newest relevant agent catalog, `SKILL.md` files, instructions, and domain workflows.
   - Give priority to current guidance from research, quant analysis, risk, backtest review, optimization, portfolio management, market structure, feature engineering, monitoring, and reporting.

2. **Research current context**
   - Gather the latest developments across markets, AI, macroeconomics, securities finance, quantitative finance, machine learning, market structure, and technology.
   - Prefer primary or authoritative sources and current quantitative data.
   - Separate facts, market reactions, and speculation.

3. **Generate quant-native angles**
   - Convert developments into non-obvious quantitative mechanisms, testable hypotheses, optimization problems, valuation frameworks, risk decompositions, second-order effects, and measurable variables.
   - Prefer ideas that reveal a hidden denominator, constraint, state variable, distributional effect, feedback loop, or opportunity-cost tradeoff.
   - Avoid generic summaries and generic AI commentary.

4. **Challenge every thesis**
   - Stress-test causality and distinguish correlation from attribution.
   - Check for leakage, survivorship bias, multiple testing, transaction costs, capacity, regime dependence, base-rate errors, misleading denominators, and unsupported extrapolation.
   - For major claims, include the strongest counterargument or caveat.

5. **Run a novelty check**
   - Avoid recycling recent themes unless new evidence materially changes the framing.
   - Common recurring themes that require fresh evidence or a new mechanism include CAPE, AI ROIC, agentic alpha, SPY concentration, AI capex, AI infrastructure financing, and multiple-testing risk.
   - Prefer building recognizable series while changing the evidence, mechanism, or research question.

6. **Create and rank 10–15 content ideas**
   - Include a mix of standalone posts, quote-posts, and threads.
   - Score each on expected engagement, originality, technical depth, timeliness, and production effort.
   - At least one idea per run must come from a deeper QuantSmith domain such as optimization, securities finance, portfolio construction, market microstructure, risk, or production ML.

7. **Package the top 3–5 ideas**
   - Produce publication-ready copy that respects current X free-post character limits.
   - For threads, split copy into numbered posts that can be pasted directly.
   - Include a concise title, suggested hashtags, engagement score, why it should resonate, and when useful a pinned reply or follow-up angle.
   - Favor concise hooks and avoid starting a thread with vague references such as “this problem” without naming the problem.

8. **Design visuals**
   - For each top idea, specify a chart, infographic, diagram, or meme that adds information rather than merely restating the post.
   - Include data required, axes/labels/annotations, visual takeaway, alt text, and source/caveat requirements.
   - Clearly label conceptual or illustrative visuals as such.
   - When current measured data support a chart, prefer an actual data-driven chart.
   - Use a distinctive finance/quant aesthetic without copying protected third-party branding.

9. **Run the meme desk**
   - Generate 3–5 timely meme concepts tied to current market, AI, quant, or pop-culture themes.
   - Prefer current or recently relevant meme formats when they strengthen the joke.
   - The meme must still carry a real market or quantitative insight understandable beyond a narrow quant audience.
   - Include complete caption, image concept, why it works, and engagement score.

10. **Verify before publishing**
    - Source and check every numerical or current-event claim used in top content.
    - Do not present an estimated, conceptual, or simulated value as measured data.
    - Do not overstate causality.
    - Distinguish levels from surprises, volume from flows, ticker count from risk diversification, adoption from monetization, and accounting profit from economic value when relevant.

11. **Assemble the nightly report**
    - Ranked 10–15 idea slate.
    - Top 3–5 publication-ready posts/threads.
    - Visual production queue.
    - 3–5 meme concepts.
    - End with:
      - Top 5 Posts To Publish Tomorrow
      - Top 3 Threads
      - Top 5 Visuals To Build
      - Top 3 Memes
      - Biggest Research Opportunity
      - Biggest Blind Spot Everyone Is Missing

## Hard Rules

- Every top post must contain at least one of: meaningful current data, original quantitative framing, a testable hypothesis, or a non-obvious second-order effect.
- Quantitative nuance beats sensational certainty.
- The purpose of a quote-post is to add information, not paraphrase the original.
- Treat market expectations as state variables; distinguish actual values from surprises when interpreting releases.
- Treat diversification as a risk-contribution question, not simply a holdings-count question.
- Treat AI spending as an economic problem involving utilization, persistence, cash conversion, financing, and opportunity cost—not merely a growth headline.
- Treat autonomous research as a multiple-testing problem when hypothesis generation scales dramatically.
- Prefer risk-adjusted, cost-aware, capacity-aware measures over headline metrics.
- Do not force contrarianism. The take must be surprising **and** defensible.
